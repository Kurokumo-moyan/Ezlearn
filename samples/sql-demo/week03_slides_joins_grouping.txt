--- slide 2 ---
Why joins?
Data about one thing is split across tables: employees(emp_id, name, dept_id, salary, manager_id) and departments(dept_id, name).
To answer "which department does Alice work in" we must combine rows from both tables.
--- slide 3 ---
INNER JOIN
SELECT e.name, d.name FROM employees e JOIN departments d ON e.dept_id = d.dept_id;
Only rows that have a match in both tables appear in the result.
--- slide 4 ---
LEFT (OUTER) JOIN
Keeps every row of the left table. If there is no matching row on the right, the right-hand columns are filled with NULL.
--- slide 5 ---
Join in relational algebra
R ⋈θ S = σθ (R × S)
A theta-join is a selection applied to the Cartesian product.
--- slide 6 ---
Logical query processing order
FROM → JOIN → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → DISTINCT → ORDER BY → LIMIT
The order in which you write the clauses is not the order in which they are evaluated.
--- slide 7 ---
GROUP BY and aggregates
COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX compute one value per group.
COUNT(*) counts rows; COUNT(column) counts non-NULL values of that column.
--- slide 8 ---
HAVING
HAVING filters rows before grouping.
WHERE filters groups after aggregation.
